What Is an eSIM?

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM chip built directly into your smartphone. Instead of inserting a small plastic SIM card into a tray, you download a carrier profile electronically onto your device. The eSIM performs the exact same function as a physical SIM: it stores your phone number, connects you to the network, and lets you make calls, send texts, and use mobile data.

The key difference is convenience. With an eSIM, there is no card to lose, no tray to open with a pin tool, and no store visit required. Etisalat (e&) fully supports eSIM for postpaid plans in the UAE, making it one of the easiest ways to get connected in 2026.

Key Fact

Starting with iPhone 14 (US models) and iPhone 15 globally, Apple removed the physical SIM tray entirely. These phones are eSIM-only, meaning an eSIM is not just convenient but required. Samsung and Google are following the same trend with newer models.

How to Check

On iPhone: go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM. If the option appears, your phone supports it. On Android: go to Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs > Add eSIM. Alternatively, dial *#06# and look for an EID (Embedded Identity Document) number in the list.

eSIM vs Physical SIM: Which Is Better?

Both eSIM and physical SIM connect you to the same Etisalat network with the same speeds, coverage, and call quality. The difference is in convenience, flexibility, and device compatibility. Here is a detailed comparison:

Feature eSIM Physical SIM
Activation Speed Minutes (scan QR code) Same day (store visit or delivery)
Store Visit Required No Yes (or delivery wait)
Dual SIM Support eSIM + physical SIM on same phone Needs dual SIM tray (limited models)
Risk of Loss/Damage None (embedded in phone) Card can be lost or damaged
Phone Switching Need new QR code from carrier Just move the SIM card
Travel Convenience Add local eSIM without removing home SIM Must swap cards or use dual tray
Device Compatibility Modern phones only (2018+) All phones
Network Quality Identical Identical
Environmental Impact No plastic waste Plastic card + packaging

For most people with a modern phone, eSIM is the better choice in 2026. The instant activation, dual SIM flexibility, and no risk of losing a tiny card outweigh the only real downside, which is slightly more effort when switching phones. If you frequently move your SIM between devices (for example, between a personal phone and a work phone), a physical SIM may still be more practical.

Common eSIM Issues and Fixes

eSIM Not Showing Network

After scanning the QR code, restart your phone. Go to Settings > Cellular/Mobile and make sure the Etisalat eSIM is toggled ON and set as the active data line. If it still does not connect, toggle Airplane Mode on for 30 seconds and back off.

QR Code Not Scanning

Ensure you are connected to Wi-Fi (you need internet to download the eSIM profile). Make sure the QR code image is clear and not blurry. Try increasing screen brightness on the device showing the QR code, and hold your camera steady for 3 to 5 seconds.

Cannot Add More eSIMs

Most phones support a limited number of eSIM profiles (typically 5 to 8 stored, with 1 to 2 active simultaneously). If you have reached the limit, delete old eSIM profiles you no longer use from Settings > Cellular > your old eSIM > Remove Cellular Plan.
